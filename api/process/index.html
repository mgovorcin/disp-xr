    Process¶

    Corrections and masks apply per granule, before chaining; re-referencing comes after. See Concepts.

    Parameters:

    Name Type Description Default
    ds Dataset

>>> ds = dxr.correct(raw, ["solid_earth_tide"])
>>> ds = dxr.correct(raw, None, external={"troposphere": tropo})

    remove_plate_motion ¶

    remove_plate_motion(ds: Dataset, static: Dataset, plate: str) -> xr.Dataset

    Subtract rigid plate motion from displacement.

    Each epoch loses v_los * (time - reference), where the reference is the granule's reference_time before :func:disp_xr.chain and attrs["reference_date"] after it, so it works on either. The result is in a plate-fixed frame, comparable with plate-fixed GNSS.

    Raises:

    Type Description

    Examples:

>>> fixed = dxr.remove_plate_motion(ds, static, "NOAM")

    mask ¶

    mask(ds: Dataset, preset: Optional[str] = 'recommended', **criteria: Any) -> xr.Dataset

    Set displacement to NaN where a pixel fails the quality criteria.

    Only displacement and short_wavelength_displacement are masked; quality layers are kept as they are. See :func:valid_mask for presets and criteria. Every layer is per epoch, so each epoch is masked by its own granule's quality. Works before or after chaining; the zero epoch that :func:disp_xr.chain prepends has no quality layers and stays 0.

    weights ¶

    Quality weights from the DISP quality layers (PRD S2, plan task T26).

    Each layer maps to a weight between 0 and 1, and the weights combine as a weighted geometric mean, prod(w_i ** (a_i / sum(a))):

    phase_similarity, temporal_coherence (good near 1) Linear stretch clip((v - floor) / (1 - floor), 0, 1): values at or below the floor get weight 0 and exclude the pixel at that epoch. timeseries_inversion_residuals (good near 0) The per-date sum of absolute residuals, in radians, of dolphin's L1 time-series inversion over every interferogram that includes the date. An unwrapping error of one cycle in one interferogram adds about 2 pi to both of its dates, so on real granules the residuals cluster near multiples of 2 pi (residual_cycle_histogram). The residual splits into cycles = round(r / 2 pi) and the distance to that lattice frac = |r / 2 pi - cycles| (0 to 0.5). The weight is penalty ** cycles * exp(-frac / f0), clamped to min_residual so a cycle jump down-weights a pixel but never removes it (PRD decision). A frac near 0.5 is ambiguous between two cycle counts and is flagged.

    The per-epoch weight (time, y, x) combines the per-epoch layer weights; the static weight (y, x) combines their time means, with the residual term replaced by one minus the share of dates that have a cycle jump.

    Defaults for the floors, penalty, f0 and min_residual are provisional until the trade study (plan task T29).

    network_degree(ds: Dataset, max_bandwidth: int) -> xr.DataArray

    Return the interferogram count of each epoch's date in its ministack.

    Bases: str, Enum

    Velocity for every pixel¶

    Velocity for every pixel: OLS, Whittle colored noise, or MIDAS.

    fit_velocity(ds, method=...) runs per pixel on a full frame:

    "ols" The linear term of :func:~disp_xr.timeseries.trajectory.fit_trajectory, with white-noise sigmas (too small for real InSAR series). "whittle" The same OLS velocity, with noise estimated per pixel from the periodogram of the OLS residuals (Whittle likelihood, power law plus white): kappa, the white fraction and amplitudes, and the colored-noise sigma of the velocity. Reliable from about 200 epochs; on short records it underestimates |kappa| and the sigma (benchmarks/whittle_bias.py). The sigma uses the covariance of the complete record, so it is approximate for pixels with gaps, and the amplitudes come from the gap-filled grid, which interpolation smooths (about 10% low with 20% gaps). The exact grouped GLS is plan task T23. "midas" The median of slopes between epoch pairs about one year apart, trimmed at two scaled MADs (Blewitt et al., 2016): robust to outliers, seasonal signals and steps, with its own sigma. A step biases it when the pairs that span it are a large share, about one year over the record length, so prefer records of several years. Pairs are chosen from the stack dates; a pair with a NaN end is dropped, where per-series MIDAS would choose a replacement.

    Blewitt, G., Kreemer, C., Hammond, W. C., & Gazeaux, J. (2016). MIDAS robust trend estimator for accurate GPS station velocities without step detection. JGR Solid Earth, 121, 2054-2068. https://doi.org/10.1002/2015JB012552

    fit_velocity ¶

    fit_velocity(obj: Union[Dataset, DataArray], method: Literal['ols', 'whittle', 'midas', 'gls'] = 'whittle', *, terms: Optional[Trajectory] = None, layer: str = 'displacement', min_valid: int = 0, noise: str = 'PLWN', groups: Union[str, DataArray] = 'coherence', n_groups: int = 5, n_samples: int = 16, allow_denoised: bool = False) -> xr.Dataset

    Estimate a velocity and its sigma for every pixel.

    Parameters:

    Name Type Description Default
    obj Dataset or DataArray

    Chained displacement (time, y, x), or a Dataset holding it.

    		 required
    method ('whittle', 'gls', 'ols', 'midas')

    "whittle" (default): colored-noise sigmas, reliable from about 200 epochs (6.5 years at 12 days); a warning is logged below. "midas": robust to unmodelled steps and outliers, for records of several years. "ols": fast, but its sigma ignores correlated noise and is several times too small. For shorter records use :func:disp_xr.fit_noise on selected pixels. See the guide "Choosing a velocity method" in the documentation.

    		 "whittle"
    terms Trajectory

    Deterministic model for "ols" and "whittle" (default offset, velocity and annual cycle). MIDAS needs none.

    		 None
    layer str

    Layer of a Dataset.

    		 'displacement'
    min_valid int

    Fewest valid epochs to fit a pixel ("ols", "whittle", "gls").

    		 0
    noise ('PLWN', 'FNWN', 'RWFNWN', 'WN', 'auto')

    Noise model of "gls"; "auto" picks FNWN, PLWN or RWFNWN per group by the median BIC of its sampled pixels.

    gls ¶

    Full-frame velocity by generalized least squares with colored noise (T23).

    The noise covariance of a pixel depends on the epochs, which of them are valid, and the noise parameters, not on the pixel's values. So the covariance shape (the spectral index and the mix of noise components) is estimated per group of pixels, by exact maximum likelihood (:func:~disp_xr.timeseries.noise.fit_noise) on a sample of each group's pixels, and every pixel is then fitted by exact GLS under its group's shape, with its own noise amplitude profiled from its residuals. Each pixel gets a colored-noise velocity sigma without an n x n factorization per pixel.

    Groups are quantile bins of time-averaged temporal_coherence by default, or any integer label map (for example quadtree cells, plan task T28).

    Missing epochs. Each group's covariance over the complete record is inverted once, Q = C⁻¹. For a pixel missing the epochs m, the inverse covariance of its valid epochs is the Schur complement P = Q - Q[:, m] Q[m, m]⁻¹ Q[m, :], which is zero on the missing rows and columns. The normal equations and residual quadratic forms follow from Q, Q A and a k x k inverse per pixel (k = len(m)), batched over pixels with the same k.

    Model diagnostics¶

    Per-pixel diagnostics of a fitted trajectory model (PRD S4, plan T32.6b-c).

    :func:model_diagnostics describes the residuals of every pixel's fit: mean, standard deviation, skewness, excess kurtosis, autocorrelation at chosen lags and the Anderson-Darling statistic for normality. White-noise sigmas (the sigma of :func:disp_xr.fit_trajectory) hold only when the residuals are uncorrelated and Gaussian; autocorrelation above about 0.3 or an Anderson-Darling statistic above about 0.75 (the 5% critical value) says they are optimistic, and :func:disp_xr.fit_velocity with a colored-noise method is the better tool. The model is evaluated on the fly from the coefficients (:func:disp_xr.predict_trajectory), never stored, and every statistic comes from one pass per block of pixels over time-contiguous chunks.

    :func:significance turns coefficients and sigmas into z-scores, two-sided p-values and confidence intervals.

    The statistics follow the Brisbane notebook (branch _stats_analysis.py), with Anderson-Darling vectorized over pixels in NumPy instead of a per-pixel apply_ufunc(vectorize=True).

    Time grids, gaps and breaks¶

    Regular time grids, refused gaps and declared breaks (PRD S3, plan task T30).

    Rules from the temporal-denoising study (Kiritimati DISP-S1, 2026-09):

    • Grid step. 15 d where sampling is genuinely 12-day, 30 d for 24-day sampling. A 15-day grid is finer than 24-day sampling supports. The step is chosen from the 75th percentile of the acquisition intervals rather than the median: the study's pre segment has a 12-day median (18 of its 34 intervals) but 14 intervals of 24 days, and the study recommends 30 d for such frames. The step is 15 d per 12 d of that percentile, rounded.
    • Gaps are refused, not interpolated. A grid point farther than max_gap_days / 2 from every acquisition lies inside a hole longer than max_gap_days and is marked; a 15-day grid across pre's 288-day hole would otherwise invent about 19 samples.
    • Breaks are declared (earthquake catalogs, user input); no automatic detection before 1.0. A break at tb lands on the first epoch at or after it: the first observation that already contains the jump. Filters that know nothing about discontinuities respect them when run per segment (:func:apply_segmented).

    Temporal denoising¶

    Temporal denoising: gated Kalman smoother and robust LOWESS (PRD S3, T31).

    Both methods share one interface, :func:denoise, and evaluate on the acquisitions or on a regular grid (:func:disp_xr.timeseries.regular_time_grid) with refused gaps. They are for display, resampling, linearity checks and transient hunting: the temporal-denoising study (Kiritimati DISP-S1, 2026-09) found that no filter improves a velocity, because InSAR residuals at 12-day sampling are white and ordinary least squares is already optimal. Fit rates on the raw series; :func:disp_xr.fit_trajectory refuses data marked as denoised.

    Kalman (method="kalman"): a local-linear-trend model (displacement and velocity, an integrated random walk) with a Rauch-Tung-Striebel smoother, or a fixed-lag smoother for products that publish as data arrive. The only knobs are the timescale tau_days (20 to 30 d), which sets the process noise q = 3 R / tau³ against the measurement variance R (estimated per pixel from the data), and the χ² gate: an update whose normalized innovation squared exceeds gate (9) is skipped. A one-epoch outlier makes one huge innovation and is rejected; a real transient builds over several epochs that stay inside the gate. In the study it left 0.2 to 0.4% of an injected spike and kept 93% of a 60-day transient. Declared breaks inflate the covariance at the first epoch after them (break_mode: offset, rate or both), and the gate is suspended there, so a real step is kept; the inflated covariance also stops the backward pass at the break.

    Unlike the study, each pixel keeps its own 2 x 2 covariance: DISP pixels have their own missing epochs, and the study's production notes ask for a per-pixel gate (unwrapping jumps) rather than its scene-wide one.

    LOWESS (method="lowess"): robust local-linear regression (tricube kernel of bandwidth_days, 60 d, and iterations bisquare reweightings, 2, per pixel), defined at any time. Its outlier rejection depends on sampling density (0.1% of a spike survives at 12-day sampling, about 30% at 24-day). Breaks are handled by fitting each segment separately.

    Known limitation: both methods were evaluated on one flat, aseismic site (Kiritimati). A deforming site with topography-correlated troposphere is part of the T29 trade-study frames.

    Colored noise (one series)¶

    Colored-noise trend estimation for one time series (maximum likelihood).

    The deterministic model (a :class:~disp_xr.timeseries.trajectory.Trajectory or any design matrix) is fitted by generalized least squares inside the likelihood of a noise model, and the noise parameters are estimated by (restricted) maximum likelihood. The resulting sigmas account for temporal correlation, which ordinary least squares ignores: for GNSS and InSAR series OLS velocity sigmas are often 5 to 10 times too small.

    Noise models:

    "WN" White noise only (equivalent to OLS). "FNWN" Flicker (spectral index -1) plus white. "PLWN" Power law with a free spectral index kappa plus white. "RWFNWN" Random walk (-2), flicker and white.

    Each likelihood evaluation factorizes an n x n covariance (O(n³)), so this is for one series or a group of pixels; full frames use the vectorized estimators of plan task T22. method="whittle" estimates the noise from the periodogram of the OLS residuals (O(n log n)) and makes one exact GLS solve.

    The method follows Hector / HectorP (Bos et al.), written from the papers:

    • Bos, M. S., Fernandes, R. M. S., Williams, S. D. P., & Bastos, L. (2013). Fast error analysis of continuous GNSS observations with missing data. Journal of Geodesy, 87(4), 351-360. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00190-012-0605-0
    • Williams, S. D. P. (2003). The effect of coloured noise on the uncertainties of rates estimated from geodetic time series. Journal of Geodesy, 76(9-10), 483-494. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00190-002-0283-4
    • Hosking, J. R. M. (1981). Fractional differencing. Biometrika, 68(1), 165-176. https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/68.1.165

    Downsampling¶

    downsample ¶

    Quality-weighted downsampling to a regular coarse grid (PRD S2, plan task T27).

    Each output cell aggregates a factor x factor block of 30 m pixels:

    • float layers: the weighted mean (stat="wmean") or weighted median (stat="wmedian"), with the weighted spread, the effective sample count n_eff = (Σw)² / Σw² and sigma = spread / sqrt(n_eff);
    • integer mask and label layers: the most common value ("mode") or whether any pixel is set ("any"), never a mean.

    With spread, each float layer also gets its spatial variation within the cell, all weighted with the same weights: <layer>_std (weighted standard deviation of the pixels), <layer>_rmse (weighted RMS of the pixels about the cell value; equal to the std for "wmean") and <layer>_mad (weighted median of the absolute deviations from the weighted median; times 1.4826 it estimates the std of Gaussian data).

    Every cell also gets n_valid, valid_fraction (of the full block, so edge cells padded beyond the frame show how much they hold) and quality (mean static weight). Weighted-mean levels keep sum_w and sum_w2 so a coarser mean can be rebuilt from them; a weighted median cannot, so every median level is computed from 30 m. The sigma treats pixels as independent, which is far too small at 1 to 10 km; pass a variogram model (:mod:disp_xr.residuals) to also get <layer>_sigma_corr, which accounts for spatially correlated errors.

    Odd factors (33, 67, 333 for 990 m, 2,010 m, 9,990 m) put each cell centre on a 30 m pixel centre, so the coarse transform is exact. Run after chaining and re-referencing: averaging series with different reference dates is wrong.

    quadtree ¶

    Quality-weighted quadtree downsampling (PRD S2 quadtree mode, plan task T28).

    Cells of level k cover 2**k x 2**k pixels of the 30 m grid, anchored at its first pixel, from min_level (finest) to max_level (coarsest). A cell splits into its four children while its split score exceeds a threshold and every child keeps enough reliable pixels; the cells that do not split are the leaves. Smooth areas end up in large cells and areas with structure (deformation, unwrapping errors) in small ones.

    The per-cell sums of :func:disp_xr.downsample (Σw, Σw², Σwv, Σwv², valid count) compose exactly: a level is the 2 x 2 block sum of the level below. So the data are read twice: once for the sums of the finest level (and from them every coarser level and every score), once for the values of the leaves. Leaf selection works on the small score maps, level by level, as boolean masks, with no recursion per cell.

    Split metrics (metric):

    • "displacement": RMS over epochs of the weighted spread of the pixels in the cell, in the unit of the data, so a threshold reads as "split where pixels in a cell differ by more than 5 mm";
    • "velocity": weighted spread of a per-pixel velocity (pass velocity, for example from :func:disp_xr.fit_velocity), in its unit;
    • "structure": the spread of the four children's means divided by their noise (the mean of their sigmas), both RMS over epochs: signal structure at the cell scale against noise, after the structure function of GPS Imaging (Hammond et al. 2016, https://doi.org/10.1002/2016JB013458). Unitless; split above about 2;
    • or a callable metric(level, children) -> (y, x) DataArray that gets the level's sums as a Dataset and those of its children (None at min_level).

    Plan task T29 compares the three and sets defaults.

    Return the effective sample count of a weighted mean of correlated pixels.
