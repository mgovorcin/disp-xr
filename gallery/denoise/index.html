    Denoising time series and regular time grids¶

    dxr.denoise smooths displacement series in time, for display and for resampling onto a regular grid. It has two methods with the same interface:

    • "kalman": a local-linear-trend Kalman filter with a smoother. It follows changes in rate over about tau_days, and a chi-square gate rejects outliers such as unwrapping jumps on single dates;
    • "lowess": robust locally weighted regression over bandwidth_days.

    Neither improves a velocity estimate. Fit velocities on the raw series: denoised data carry attrs["denoised"], and the fits refuse them.

    The series below has a known signal and irregular sampling like real DISP-S1 frames: 12-day revisits, some 24-day ones, and a 200-day hole. It also has a one-date spike (an unwrapping error), a step on a known date, and a short transient.

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import xarray as xr

    [np.arange(0, 408, 12), np.arange(420, 700, 24), np.arange(900, 1500, 12)]
)
times = pd.Timestamp("2018-01-01") + pd.to_timedelta(days, "D")
years = days / 365.25
truth = (
    -0.012 * years  # subsidence, 12 mm/yr
    + 0.004 * np.sin(2 * np.pi * years)  # 4 mm annual cycle
    + 0.010 * (days >= 1100)  # 10 mm step
    + 0.008 * np.exp(-(((days - 1300) / 30.0) ** 2))  # 8 mm transient
)
noisy = truth + 0.002 * rng.standard_normal((4, days.size))
noisy[:, 50] += 0.028  # unwrapping error: half a wavelength on one date
series = xr.DataArray(
    noisy.T[:, :, None],
    dims=("time", "y", "x"),
    coords={"time": times, "y": np.arange(4.0), "x": [0.0]},
    attrs={"units": "meters"},
)
intervals = sorted({int(d) for d in np.diff(days)})
print(f"{days.size} acquisitions, intervals {intervals} days")

    On the acquisitions¶

    The step is declared with breaks=. Automatic break detection is out of scope: steps come from catalogs, such as earthquakes or equipment changes.

def rms_mm(a):
    """Return the RMS of ``a`` in millimeters."""
    return 1e3 * float(np.sqrt(np.nanmean(np.square(a))))

    Both remove the spike: the Kalman gate rejects the date, and LOWESS down-weights it through its robust passes. Both keep the step, because it is declared. They differ on the transient, a signal of about two months: the Kalman filter keeps more of it than the 60-day LOWESS window. A shorter tau_days or bandwidth_days keeps faster signals at the cost of more noise.

    On a regular grid¶

    grid="auto" resamples onto a regular grid. Its step comes from the sampling: 15 days for 12-day revisits, 30 days when 24-day intervals are common. Grid dates inside holes longer than max_gap_days are refused (NaN), not interpolated.

    Denoised data are for display¶

    The output records the method, and the fits refuse it unless asked explicitly. Smoothing correlates the residuals, so velocity sigmas from a denoised series would be far too small.
