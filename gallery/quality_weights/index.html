    Quality weights¶

    quality_weights turns the DISP quality layers into one weight per pixel and epoch, between 0 and 1: a linear stretch of phase similarity and temporal coherence above a floor, and a down-weighting of dates whose inversion residuals show unwrapping cycle jumps (multiples of 2 pi). The weights feed weighted fits and, later, quality-weighted downsampling.

    Each layer and the combination¶

    Epoch 1 has injected cycle jumps at two pixels (one and two cycles), and a low-coherence corner. The combined weight is the geometric mean of the layer weights, so a pixel is only trusted when every layer is good.

    Cycle jumps down-weight, never exclude¶

    Each 2 pi cycle multiplies the residual weight by penalty (0.5), and the weight never drops below min_residual (0.05). Low coherence or similarity, below the processor's thresholds, does exclude (weight 0).

    Weighted fit¶

    Weights enter fit_trajectory as 1 / variance factors; a zero weight drops the epoch, so the excluded corner gets no velocity.

    Copied! 
    stacked = dxr.chain(ds)
weighted = dxr.fit_trajectory(
    stacked, weights=dxr.quality_weights(stacked).weight.fillna(0)
)
plain = dxr.fit_trajectory(stacked)
fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(9, 3.8), layout="constrained")
for ax, fit, title in zip(axes, (plain, weighted), ("unweighted", "quality-weighted")):
    (fit.velocity * 1000).plot.imshow(
        ax=ax, cmap="RdBu_r", cbar_kwargs={"label": "mm/yr"}
    )
    ax.set_title(title, loc="left")
    ax.set_aspect("equal")
plt.show()

    Do residuals really cluster at 2 pi?¶

    residual_cycle_histogram counts residuals in units of 2 pi. On real DISP-S1 granules (frame F44055) the counts peak near 1, 2 and 3 cycles, which is what the cycle decomposition assumes; run it on your own frame to check.
